#### IIBA certifications & certificates ---
  1. ECBA® –
    Entry Certificate in Business Analysis
  2. CCBA® –
    Certification of Capability in Business Analysis
  3. CBAP® –
    Certified Business Analysis Professional
  4. Specialized certificates -
    including IIBA-AAC – Agile Analysis Certification
John's take:
Things to consider include when investing in certification are if you are the target audience, the TCO ( including your time spent ), and the potential value for your career
#### What these certificates are (and are not) ---
  1. Globally recognized
  2. Demonstrates commitment to professional growth
  3. Validation of knowledge and experience
  4. Shared professional language
  5. Career signal — not a guarantee
  6. Not a substitute for practice
John's take:
Once earned, these are great conversation starters in, for example, interviews, and they also establish yourself as a professional. Real job experience is better over certifications alone.
### The IIBA certification process ---
  1. Choose the right certification
  2. Meet eligibility requirements
  3. Prepare and study
  4. Apply and schedule
  5. Take the exam
  6. Maintain your certification
John's take:
Just be aware that there are many steps before you take an exam. They don't necessarily have to be executed in order.
#### Step 1: Choose the right certification ---
  1. Match certification to role and experience
  2. Generalist vs dual role or specialization
  3. Depth vs breadth
John's take:
Pick the cert that reflects your current work — not your aspirational title. My specific goals are to be an IIBA Academic Partner or Training Partner. I'm testing the journey.
#### Step 2: Meet eligibility requirements ---
  1. Experience hours
  2. Professional development
  3. References (where required)
John's take:
IMO, some of these hour counts are a little too granular (e.g. 3750 hours). My advice is to familiarize yourself with the requirements and make this an ongoing activity BEFORE you decide to take an exam. When you get a job, keep this up to date if you have any desire to take a Core Certification. Note that 7,500 hours is roughly equivalent to 3.5 - 4 years of full-time work. Also note that CCBA and CBAP might require an audit before you can take the exam. Audits are conducted on a random basis.

#### Step 3: Prepare and study ---
  1. Experience in a BA role
  2. The BABOK® Guide v3 (Business Analysis Body of Knowledge)
  3. The Business Analysis Competency Model®
  4. Sample questions
John’s take:
I used this order and interchangeably to compliment one practice for another. IIBA members get access to BABOK® and the Competency Model. Some other test takers have gotten good value from Watermark Exam Simulator (fee-based)
Demo: IIBA-AAC Sample questions
#### Step 4: Apply and schedule ---
  1. IIBA application review (when applicable)
  2. Approval window - 1 year
  3. Purchase the exam (agree to terms) - good for 6 months
  4. PSI scheduling
John's take:
Plan this step like a project milestone — not a formality. PSI is the third-party testing provider that IIBA partners with to securely deliver, proctor, and score IIBA certification exams worldwide. For online exams, be familiar with the PSI Online Proctoring Experience, and what you are and are not allowed to have during your exam. You can only take the online or in-person test during your scheduled time. You will most likely get your preferred exam session time 4-6 weeks in advance.
Demo: Scheduling the exam
#### Step 5: Take the exam ---
  1. Multiple-choice, scenario-based, single-answer questions.
  2. Time-boxed
  3. Best answer mindset
John's take:
It seems like the questions themselves are more straightforward than the preparation to take the exam. Equipment for online protored exams include a web cam, speakers, microphone, and stable broadband internet connection. Work computers are not allowed to be used for testing. Watch the brief video on the PSI Online Proctored Experience
#### Step 6: Maintain your certification ---
  1. Continuing Development Units (CDUs)
  2. Three-year cycle
  3. Be aware of the recertification process and fees
John's take:
To maintain the credential, continuing competence must be demonstrated every 3 years through 60 Continuing Development Units (CDUs). ECBA is the exemption to the standard CDU model - just retake the exam. Enter CDUs into your BA Development Log. See the appropriate RECERTIFICATION HANDBOOK to ensure CDU activity alignedment.
#### My IIBA-AAC experience ---
  • Overall: I'm still trying to weight the investment with value. Is the IIBA-AAC only really worth investing in my career?
  • I'm very impressed with the level of professionalism, amont of information available, support, and recognition with IIBA certs
  • The preparation, although well-structured, seems overwhelming
  • A company that invests in Business Analysis should make this part of their training program
  • Recertification is a bit of a pain, but 3 years is not a huge ask
  • The IIBA site has loads of information. Almost too much. Hopefully this presentation will give you a good start
