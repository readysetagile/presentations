John's take:
We are moving from a single mentor model to a scalable talent platform.
How the Tax breaks down in the model: The 50% Drain: In most SME IT environments, a senior developer or architect spends roughly 50% of their work week reviewing, refactoring, or completely rewriting junior contributions. If that senior makes $150k, you are essentially paying $75k per year just to "fix" the output of one junior hire. The AI Multiplier: Without disciplined engineering methods, juniors use AI to produce high volumes of code that looks correct but lacks structural integrity. This forces the senior into a high-stress "infinite review loop," which is a primary driver of senior developer burnout. The Opportunity Cost: Every hour a senior spends as a "human compiler" for a junior is an hour they aren't spent on innovation, architecture, or high-value business logic. Why NextGen Talent Lab solves it: NextGen eliminates the Review Tax by moving the "holding hands" phase out of the employer's office and into the 16-week mentorship lab. By the time a candidate reaches the employer, they have been trained to use Disciplined Engineering—meaning they produce code that is already at a production-ready standard. For the employer, this shifts the $75k "Review Tax" back into $75k of senior-level innovation. Why this works: By labeling the Status Quo with "Vibe Coding" and "Review Tax," you are using the terminology that your survey identified as high-pain points for managers. It shifts the conversation from "training costs" to "operational waste," making your $30k price point look like a cost-saving measure rather than an expense.
